Strategic Evaluation and Architectural Roadmap for the JustInTime Service Marketplace: Identifying Functional Gaps in the Kenyan Gig Economy ContextThe digital transformation of the Kenyan service sector has reached a critical inflection point where the mere facilitation of a connection between a service provider and a consumer is no longer a sufficient value proposition. As the gig economy in Kenya is projected to reach a valuation of $23 billion by 2025, accounting for over 9% of the national GDP, the competitive landscape for platforms like JustInTime is shifting from basic matching to comprehensive ecosystem management. While JustInTime possesses a robust core—including hyperlocal matching, M-Pesa integration, and AI-powered matching—it operates in an environment increasingly defined by aggressive fiscal regulation, such as the 2025 Finance Bill, and a heightening demand for physical safety and professional back-office tools. This report provides an exhaustive gap analysis, identifying the missing features required to transition JustInTime from a transactional utility to a market-leading SaaS-Enabled Marketplace (SEMA).Comparative Benchmarking against Global Industry LeadersTo understand the trajectory of JustInTime, it is necessary to analyze the feature sets of global incumbents like TaskRabbit, Thumbtack, and Angi. Conversely, TaskRabbit utilizes a commission-based intermediary model that focuses on instant booking and hourly rates, charging a trust and support fee to cover background checks and insurance. This change subjects digital work to excise duty, an instrument traditionally reserved for goods with negative externalities. For the 1.9 million Kenyans engaged in digital labor, this adds a layer of complexity that JustInTime is currently unequipped to handle.A critical missing feature is an "Excise Duty Calculation Engine" within the integrated wallet. This engine would automatically calculate and separate the required excise duty from the total transaction value, providing clear transparency to both the worker and the client.
